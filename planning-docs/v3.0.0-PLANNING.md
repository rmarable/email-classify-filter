## 1. Why

- **Local speed under load.** On the fanless development Mac, Gemma 4 12B drops from about 26 to
  11-16 tokens/s within minutes of sustained work; one email takes about 3.8-5.7 s cool. The
  thermal probe has recorded nothing in any run (`{}` in all 29 archived results), so heat as the
  cause is measured indirectly, not confirmed (P17). Ollama MLX is not a fix today: the HTTP 501
  on structured output was the Homebrew build lacking `libollama_xgrammar.dylib`; Ollama's own
  0.40.3 build has it, and with `format` on both, MLX was 8% faster over a 60-email backlog (249
  s against 271 s) and not significantly different in accuracy (82.2% against 83.3% of fields,
  all-right cards 132 against 132, McNemar p 1.0), with more swap (SPEC §1.2, §21.2 at
  `5868001`; a trade-off, not adopted).
- **Claude already does better.** On the frozen set (268 cards), preset C scores 223 (83.2%,
  `61686db4`) against Gemma's 183 (68.3%, `d80ea43d`), both 0 unsafe and recall 95/95 (SPEC
  §21.2).
- **Long-term goal** (operator): optimise on Jev-style classifier modelling (typed questions with
  probabilities), then go local again. Superseded 2026-10-14: local models are removed (ADR 0031);
  Bedrock is the path to revisit if TypeSafe offers Jev there.

## 2. Direction (operator decisions 2026-10-12)

- **Preset C (all-Claude) becomes the default.** Claude stays on demand: mail waits for an
  `/ecf-review` session (P3; ADR 0017). No unattended Claude in this release.
- **C is Claude-only** (operator 2026-10-12): no Gemma fallback and no Gemma check.
  `claude_queue_timeout` can no longer be enabled on a C address, and its fallback shadow runs
  and gate don't apply to C (amends OD-019 and OD-020 for C; an OD when built). Mail on a C
  address waits for a review session however long that takes; the model-free pre-check and rules
  still flag fraud and regulator mail. (B kept its fallback in draft 4; B and the fallback are
  removed, ADR 0031.)
- **A Jev + Claude-actor preset is built beside it.** Jev classifies when mail is fetched; the
  Claude actor runs on demand. **When Jev is down, Claude classifies** (operator 2026-10-13,
  replacing P11's "mail waits"): the item joins the Claude review queue as a C item would, so it
  is classified at the next `/ecf-review`; there is no Gemma fallback. Switch and switch-back in
  §4.3.
- **Presets A and B are removed** (operator decision 2026-10-14, OD-506, ADR 0031; was OD-495, A
  not prioritized): every A or B address becomes C and is paused at upgrade; resuming it is the
  consent act (OD-427). Plan: `planning-docs/REMOVE-LOCAL-MODELS.md`.
- **Every role gets the best model for the job** (operator 2026-10-12; CLAUDE.md, model choice):
  the larger model stays unless a smaller one (Opus, Sonnet, Haiku 5.5) is shown to match it on that job under the evaluation rules (OD-486). Splits are made
  with care (§4.1).

  The `actor` role's per-role comparison runs in step 6 (OD-517). No actor combination has been tried and rejected.

How a split is judged (operator 2026-10-12: the best model for the job, not the cheapest):

- **Per role, not per field.** One classifier call answers every field, so a role can't use Haiku
  for priority and Sonnet for category. Haiku 4.5 was better than Sonnet on priority (100% vs
  91.7%) and worse on category (68.9% vs 83.1%) and fraud risk; Haiku 5.5 in H1 was worse than
  Sonnet on priority (62.5% vs 71.2%) and category (83.1% vs 86.5%), better on requires_reply
  (93.0% vs 88.1%). A field-level win doesn't move a role.
- **Safety first, then accuracy.** The smaller model must hold the absolute gates (0 unsafe,
  full fraud-guard recall, injection cases safe) and show no significant loss on any
  safety-relevant field (fraud risk, category, rule) and no overall loss beyond the named margin.
  A significant loss on one such field keeps the larger model even if the total is within margin.
- **Differences are read, not just counted.** As in 6b (Sonnet against Opus as `actor_high`:
  same classifications, actions differed on 3 cards, the operator judged 2 equal and 1 better on
  Opus), the operator judges the cases that differ; when a difference favours the larger model on
  a high-risk item, the larger model keeps the role.
- **Noise.** Claude results vary run to run (2-3 of 10 re-classifications differ), so a split
  needs repeat runs or the paired comparison, not one score.
- **Cost and time come last:** only between models that are equal on the above.

Results set the pins per role. A pin change is a gate-key change (checked release path).
`actor_high` stays Opus until step 6 chooses its pin (was v3.0.1; folded into v3.0.0 by the
operator 2026-10-15, OD-517), after the operator judged the cases that differ in H2 and H2b. The
`actor` role can't be pinned from a measurement until a set with sender history exists (H4 has the
same gap).

**Tier ladder so far** (superseded 2026-10-15 by step 6: every gate role on Opus 5.5, OD-520,
OD-521; the table records what was known before step 6) (operator 2026-10-12: "Haiku could be good enough where we use Sonnet, and
Sonnet where we use Opus"; checked against the runs):

| Role | Pin | Smaller model measured | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| `classifier`, `classifier_high` | Sonnet | Haiku 5.5, H1 | Significant loss on `requires_action`: Sonnet stays. |
| `actor_high` | Opus | Sonnet, 6b (`82106673` vs `fccf0c0a`, preset B, same Gemma classifications in both arms) | Same safety results; 3 actions differ, operator judged Sonnet no worse overall; $0.81 vs $1.57. |
| `actor_high` | Opus | Haiku 5.5, H2 and H2b (vs `61686db4`, C) | 0 unsafe, no hide on any injection card; H2: 9 actions differ (5 added escalations, 3 dropped flags, 1 leave); H2b: 6 of those recur, including the flag dropped on `inj-subject-line`, plus 3 new; $0.05 vs $1.61. |

Reading: acting is constrained by the classification, rules and policy (the actor chooses among a
few allowed actions, and policy checks them again), so a smaller model plausibly suffices there;
classifying is the harder, safety-relevant job. Sonnet's `actor_high` result is one run; Haiku's
is two (H2, H2b), and actions are judged by eye on the cases that differ. Next: the operator
judges Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet against Opus side by side on their differing cases (for Haiku, the 6
that recur first, `inj-subject-line` above all) and picks the `actor_high` pin in this release (a
gate-key change, checked path). That replaces "until its own revisit after this release" if the
operator agrees. **Done 2026-10-14:** the operator judged the differing cases and moved the choice to
v3.0.1, immediately after this release (`planning-docs/v3.0.1-PLANNING.md`); Opus stays here. On 2026-10-15 that moved into this release as step 6 (OD-517).

### 4.3 The Jev classifier

- **What the docs settle** (2026-10-13; schema doc §4): (a) fixed versions exist (`jev-1.13.0`;
  aliases `jev-latest`, `jev-preview`; /models); (b) the response's `model` field reports the
  versioned ID that answered (/models, /api); (c) Jev has per-question `instructions` and
  `criteria` and no request-wide instruction field (/api). Endpoint `POST
  https://api.typesafe.ai/v1/systemone`, Bearer auth; body `model`, `state`, `questions`;
  response `model`, `answers`, `usage`. 64k tokens per request, 32k for state plus the longest
  question; 100K tokens/s, 80 requests/s.
- **Precondition (step 2 starts here):** one live response (synthetic data, operator go-ahead)
  confirms (b) and finds what Jev returns for an over-size request (undocumented). Anything that
  differs from the docs is reported to the operator; nothing is worked around automatically.
- **Adapter:** A new module `ecf_server/jev.py` holds the hosted transport (plain HTTPS to
  api.typesafe.ai, the remote httpx pattern in `model_watch.py:82-85`), the pin, the error classes
  and the circuit breaker that becomes the Jev-down signal; it imports `systemone`'s question
  builder and answer mapper, and nothing moves out of `systemone.py`. `classifier` never imports
  `jev` (an import-linter contract); step 4 dispatches in a new module (D4, P9, OD-503; ADR 0031).
  Not TypeSafe's Python
  SDK: it reads `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` from the environment when no key is passed, defaults to
  `jev-latest`, and `TYPESAFE_LOG_LEVEL=debug` logs request and response bodies
  (/sdk/python/usage). Jev replaces the classify step only; facts, triggers, rules, policy,
  stage, approvals and step-up are unchanged, and this is a release criterion (P4).
- **Payload:** what the Claude classifier gets: the sender's display name and address, the
  subject, the date (normalised), attachment metadata, each on one line and checked for injection
  with redaction on, and the prepared, redacted excerpt (1,500 characters) (OD-504, D1, P10), the
  questions for the fixed set, the default fields (unless off) and the user's fields (schema
  doc §1), and the derived signals in fixed wording (OD-483). Nothing else. Ordinal levels carry
  their descriptions (`score` criteria accept them; today `systemone.py:95` sends names only).
  A test bounds the request size. The exact payload is written into SPEC §12.4 before ship
  (§4.8).
- **Pin:** `jev-1.13.0` (or the version current at build), in a lock file shipped in the wheel.
  The response's model is checked on every call; a mismatch refuses the answer. TypeSafe states
  no retirement policy, so a pinned version that disappears fails closed: mail waits and
  `ecf doctor` says why. The weekly model watch records the Jev aliases' `release_date` from
  `GET /v1/models` in step 4 (STEP5 §8; JEV-STEP2 §5.3: the listing shows aliases only, so it
  can't see a pinned version retire). A pin change restarts gates (P10).
- **Key:** `jev-api-key` in the OS secret store, written by the service (`ecf models api-key set --provider typesafe`
  through its own route, and an `ecf init` step in step 4, validated by one minimal call with
  no email content; a Security Notice on every set, change or clear; OD-503); covered by
  regrant and destroy; never in backups; never `$TYPESAFE_API_KEY` (P13). In AWS mode (M1) it goes into AWS Secrets Manager
  (operator 2026-10-13; a note for the M1 roadmap doc).
- **Failures:** 429, 529 (overloaded), other 5xx and timeouts are provider faults: no attempt
  counted, the item retries Jev until the switch below. 401 raises a System Error (bad key) and
  counts as Jev down. 400, 404, 413 and 422 are request errors (not retried) and an answer that
  doesn't map is a failed attempt; both are scored as failures, never a default (OD-503, P1). The
  Ollama decision-model path and its HTTP 400 handling are removed (ADR 0031).
  Score ties resolve toward more risk; a choice outside the options is a failed attempt
  (`systemone.py`, `map_answers`). Timeout and retry are set from Jev's measured latency (e.g. p99 plus a stated margin)
  in step 3, with the source recorded; SPEC §15.4 gets the row (P12).
- **Jev down: switch to Claude** (operator 2026-10-13; proposal, figures set in step 3):
  - *Per call:* a request times out after p99 latency plus a margin (P12).
  - *Switch:* an address marks Jev **down** when its calls have failed with provider faults
    (or 401) for `jev_down_after_minutes` (proposed default 15, range 5-1440, an address
    setting; no step-up, since it only moves work to another allowed classifier). While down,
    new mail and items waiting for Jev skip Jev and go to the Claude queue (`awaiting_claude`,
    need `classify`), classified by the C classifier pin under C's gate key, which must have
    passed for that address (else the item waits and `ecf doctor` says why). The card says
    "classified by Claude (Jev down)". Failed attempts (422, an answer that doesn't map) don't
    switch; after the usual attempt limit the item goes to Claude too.
  - *Switch back:* while down, Jev's circuit-breaker probe, a minimal POST with no email content
    (the key-check call), runs at each cool-down end (30 s doubling to 5 min) and is the
    switch-back check (JEV-STEP2 K2b, D11, P7). The first success clears **down**; new mail goes to Jev again, and items
    still waiting in the Claude queue and not yet claimed go back to Jev (as OD-293 returns
    items when the fallback is turned off). Claimed or classified items stay with Claude; nothing
    is classified twice.
  - Slack: one notice when Jev goes down (count, oldest age, "run `/ecf-review` to classify
    them") and one on recovery (§4.11). Audited. Privacy: Claude is already an allowed
    destination, so nothing new.
  - An OD records this (replaces P11 for the Jev preset).
- **Confidence:** Jev's probabilities are recorded for calibration. They may only make ecf more
  cautious: the middle band on the yes/no fixed items flags and blocks hiding, and a rule that
  hides mail on a choice field acts only above a confidence bar (schema doc §4; a new OD amends
  the OD-054 wording for this direction). They never route an item to a weaker model or lower a
  risk (P26).
- **Logging:** `model_calls` gains a backend column and the Jev model ID (step 4; 0018's role
  CHECK needs a rebuild migration for it, numbered after 0039, which step 5 takes; STEP5 §8); TypeSafe's request ID (the `x-typesafe-request-id`
  header) goes in the service log only, never `model_calls` or result files (JEV-STEP2 D7, K3);
  numbers only (`usage` tokens, client wall time, network time where separable);
  never request or response bodies (P22; OD-165). Field names Jev uses for content (`state`,
  `instructions`, `criteria`) join the log scrubber's list.

### 4.4 The Jev evaluation (OD-486)

- Through `ecf eval run` on the frozen set, answering every schema field, with the same signals
  block as the other arms (P8).
- **Prompt digest:** SHA-256 of the preamble, the full question JSON, the excerpt and header caps,

## 7. Limits

- C keeps mail waiting for a review session, with no local fallback however long it waits (a
  reminder at 24 h, a loud alert at 48 h); only the Jev preset classifies on fetch. Addresses
  converted from A lose classification at every check (removal P18).
- Jev's latency, accuracy and determinism are unmeasured; its base model and a version
  retirement policy are unpublished.
- Jev's known weaknesses (/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13): literal reading of negation, dates read
  as text, weak score calibration, a lean toward the first option, injected text not treated as
  hostile by default, lower accuracy as unrelated state grows. Other languages, CJK included,
  are "handled but not equally well" (/models): a stated limit for non-English mail.
- The synthetic set is public in the repo; whether Jev's training saw it is unknown.
- Claude results vary run to run (2-3 of 10 re-classifications differ; no temperature).
- Actions have no scored measure: the eval scores classifications and rules, and action
  differences are judged by the operator case by case.
- C's coverage of model-only scams and injection hides (§4.7) is from one frozen-set run.
- Costs in SPEC are API-equivalent; Claude runs on the reviewer's plan allowance (SPEC §20).
